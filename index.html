About Me

I am a Senior Applied Scientist at Oracle, where I work on agentic AI systems. My research focuses on building reliable and verifiable AI agents. In particular, I study how increasingly capable models can reason, act, and interact with complex environments over long-horizon tasks while remaining grounded in explicit structure and constraints. I am especially interested in rigorous evaluation of agent behavior and in using executable feedback, structured representations, and verification to make agents more reliable. I completed my Ph.D. in Computer and Information Science at the University of Pennsylvania in 2026, advised by Mayur Naik.

Research Interests

I am generally interested in three areas of research:

Reliable Agentic Reasoning

I am interested in how to make AI agents reason and act reliably over long-horizon tasks. As agents interact with tools, retrieve information, and make decisions over many steps, errors in grounding, reasoning, or intermediate actions can easily compound. My work has explored different ways of making such reasoning more reliable, from SQRL, which extracts statistical rules and constraints from data, to recent work on verifying model-generated mathematical proofs. Going forward, I am interested in agents that can remain grounded throughout long-horizon execution, reason under explicit constraints, and use verification to detect errors before they propagate.

AI for Code

Code is a natural setting for studying reliable agents because programs have rich structure, can be executed, and often admit precise notions of correctness. My work in this area has explored how models can reason over structured representations of programs, as in CodeTrek, use executable feedback during generation, as in TiCoder, and interact with formal verification, as in Code2Inv. I am interested in extending these ideas to coding agents that operate over larger codebases and longer trajectories, where agents must reason about program structure, use tools, incorporate feedback, and verify the changes they make.

Neurosymbolic AI Systems

My work has explored ways to combine the flexibility of neural models with the structure and guarantees provided by symbolic reasoning. I have developed systems such as Dolphin, for integrating symbolic reasoning directly into neural learning, and TorchQL, for expressing and checking constraints over machine learning systems. I am interested in building scalable neurosymbolic systems in which foundation models can interact directly with structured state, executable specifications, and verification procedures, and in using these abstractions as building blocks for more reliable agents.
